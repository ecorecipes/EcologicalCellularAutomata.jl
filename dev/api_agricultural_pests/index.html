Builders, problem constructors and helpers of the models whose category is "agricultural pest".

Huffaker's mites

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_huffaker_mites — Function
build_huffaker_mites() -> CAModel

The state of the mite model of (Zsoldos and Stepler, 2024): layers mite (0 empty, 1 prey, 2 predator), fertility and energy (counters of the mite in a cell) and post (whether the cell holds a wooden post). The model's rule leaves every cell unchanged: the dynamics, in which mites act one at a time, are the step callback huffaker_step_callback, which problem_huffaker_mites stores with the problem. The parameters are F_prey and F_predator (fertility thresholds), L_predator (a predator's initial energy), loss (energy lost by a predator moving without eating), gain (energy gained by eating) and prey_energy.

problem_huffaker_mites(; experiment=2, prey=120, predators=27, posts=:code, tspan=500,
                       seed=0, pmap=(;), kwargs...) -> CAProblem

One of the four experiments of (Zsoldos and Stepler, 2024), with prey prey and predators predators on distinct random cells (drawn from seed) for 500 steps:

  1. a 20 × 24 grid (four patches per orange), no posts;
  2. a 30 × 36 grid (nine patches per orange), no posts;
  3. as 2, with the 18 posts (HUFFAKER_POSTS);
  4. as 3, with the fertility thresholds, the predators' initial energy and their energy loss doubled (F_prey = 2, F_predator = 20, L_predator = 2, loss = 2).

The step callback, with the post rule posts, is stored with the problem.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.huffaker_step! — Function
huffaker_step!(u, p, rng; posts=:code) -> u

One step of the mite model of (Zsoldos and Stepler, 2024), in place on the layered state u, following the source's code. The mites alive at the start act once each, in a random order; each one's fertility rises by one; a predator with a prey among its von Neumann neighbours eats one at random and moves into its cell, gaining gain; otherwise the mite moves to a random empty neighbour, a predator losing loss, or stays if there is none. A predator whose energy reaches zero dies where it stands. A mite that moved and has reached its fertility threshold resets it and leaves an offspring in the cell it left. No cell lies beyond the edges.

Prey may also use the posts: with posts = :code (the source's code) every empty post is among a prey's possible moves, wherever it stands; with posts = :text (the source's description) a prey standing on a post may jump to a random empty post instead of a neighbour, with the jump counted as one more possible move; with posts = :none posts are ignored.

Two lepidopterans in a Bt cotton refuge

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_bt_cotton_refuge — Function
build_bt_cotton_refuge() -> CAModel

The refuge competition model of (Malaquias et al., 2021). Each cell is a non-Bt cotton plant, empty (0) or holding S. frugiperda (1) or H. armigera (2); a step is a generation, and cells update synchronously from their Moore neighbours.

An occupied cell empties with probability mortality (natural enemies and dispersal); then, in a sprayed generation (spray), with probability kill, unless its species is resistant (resistant_Sf, resistant_Ha), in which case the larva escapes with probability escape; then it survives competition with probability s. The survival table (bt_cotton_survival) is the high-density one if at least threshold * 8 neighbours are occupied, and s is the value for meeting the other species with probability n_other / 8, the share of neighbours holding it, and for the species alone otherwise.

An empty cell with n₁ and n₂ neighbours of the two species is colonised by S. frugiperda with probability f₁ = emergence n₁ R₁ / (n₁ R₁ + n₂ R₂), and failing that by H. armigera with probability f₂, defined alike, where R₁ and R₂ are the fecundities fecundity_Sf and fecundity_Ha.

spray is set by the callback bt_cotton_spray_callback, which the problem constructor stores with the problem.

Ant nests and a fungus on a coffee farm

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_coffee_ant_fungus — Function
build_coffee_ant_fungus(; mortality=:fungus, phases=:two) -> CAModel

The ant and fungus automaton of (Jackson et al., 2009). Each cell is a shade tree: empty (0), an Azteca nest whose scale insects are free of the fungus (1), or a nest whose scale insects are infected (2). A step is six months. With N occupied and F infected Moore neighbours:

  • an empty tree with N > 0 gains a satellite nest, uninfected, with probability s0 + s1 N;
  • an uninfected nest is infected with probability min(1, t0 + t1 F) if F > 0, and with the background probability a otherwise;
  • an infected nest dies with probability v (virulence); uninfected nests do not die.

With mortality = :phorid the model is the fungus-free automaton of (Vandermeer et al., 2008) that it extends: a nest dies with probability m0 + m1 N (attack by phorid flies), and nests are never infected.

The source's iteration draws colonisation for every empty tree and mortality for every nest. With phases = :two (the default) an iteration is two steps: the even step colonises, and the odd step infects and kills, counting the nests just founded among the neighbours; ant_iterations converts steps to iterations. With phases = :one both happen in one step, from the same state. Within a step the source updates "cell by cell": solve with Synchronous() (the default) or RandomSequential() for an in-place sweep.

Cassava mealybugs and green lacewings

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_cassava_mealybug — Function
build_cassava_mealybug() -> CAModel

The within-plant dynamics of the cassava mealybug model of (Promrak and Rattanakul, 2015), with the algorithm of (Promrak and Rattanakul, 2017). Each cell is a cassava plant, susceptible (0), infested (1) or removed (2), with mealybug eggs, instars and adults (Pe, Pi, Pm), lacewing eggs, larvae, pupae and adults (Me, Mi, Md, Mm), the day it was infested (since, -1 if never) and the worst yield loss it has suffered (loss: 0 none, 1 a tenth, 2 three tenths, 3 total). Each day, in every plant:

Pi' = Pi + r1 α1 Pe - α2 Pi - min(20 Mi, Pi)
Pm' = Pm + r2 α2 Pi - α3 Pm - min(20 Mi, Pm)
Pe' = Pe + r3 α4 ν1 Pm - α1 Pe - min(20 Mi, Pe)
Mi' = Mi + s1 γ1 Me - γ2 Mi
Md' = Md + s2 γ2 δ1 Mi - γ3 Md,  δ1 = 0.0521 min(1, (Pi + Pm + Pe) / (60 Mi))
Mm' = Mm + s3 γ3 Md - δ2 Mm
Me' = Me + s4 ν2 Mm - γ1 Me

(a larva eats at most 20 of each mealybug stage a day). A plant infested for two weeks or more suffers the loss of its period: total in days 1 to 120, three tenths in days 121 to 210, a tenth afterwards. Movement between plants, surveys, removal and releases are cassava_management_callback.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.cassava_sweep! — Function
cassava_sweep!(u, p, day, rng) -> (found, sampled)

One day of movement, survey and removal in the cassava mealybug model of (Promrak and Rattanakul, 2015), in place on the state u, following the flowcharts of (Promrak and Rattanakul, 2017). Plants are visited once each, in random order:

  • a susceptible plant is infested by wind (probability w1 on the two border rows, w2 inside) and, for each infested neighbour not yet visited that has an instar to give, gains it with probability n1, n2 or n3 by ring, becoming infested;
  • an infested plant whose mealybug total is not positive becomes susceptible, its counts set to zero; on a survey day (every 14 days from day 31) it is sampled with probability survey_plants over the plants not removed, and a sampled plant is removed, unless it is sampled in days 121 to 210 with at most m1 mealybugs; otherwise it gains instars by wind and from neighbours as above.

Returns the infested plants found and the plants sampled on a survey day, and (0, 0) on other days.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.cassava_release! — Function
cassava_release!(u, p, day) -> (p, larvae)

The release rule of (Promrak and Rattanakul, 2015): while the last survey found infested plants, lacewing larvae are released every interval days from the first such survey, G1 if over half the plants sampled were infested and G2 otherwise, split evenly over the infested plants. Returns the updated management memory and the larvae released.

Telenomus releases against the brown stink bug

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_telenomus_release — Function
build_telenomus_release() -> CAModel

The within-cell dynamics of the soybean field of (Weber et al., 2022), in daily steps on 1 m cells. Each cell holds female stink bugs (Euschistus heros) as counts by age, nymphs (ages 1 to 30) and adults (ages 1 to 36), up to 60 egg masses (StinkBugEggMass) in masses, and female wasps (Telenomus podisi) as counts by age in wasps (ages 1 to 26). Each day, in every cell:

  • adult stink bugs age a day (not on the first day) and die with probability mort_adult or at age 37; wasps die with probability mort_wasp or at age 26;
  • nymphs of age 30 become adults of age 1 while the cell has room (300 adults); the others age a day and die with probability mort_nymph;
  • egg masses of age 5 hatch ⌊eggs × sex_ratio × egg_survival⌋ female nymphs (3 for an intact mass of 11) while the cell has room (500 nymphs), and the others age a day; parasitoid immatures of age 14 are removed or, with offspring = true, give ⌊para × sex_ratio_wasp × immature_survival⌋ female wasps while the cell has room (550 wasps), and the others age a day.

Movement, egg laying, parasitism, colonisation and releases are telenomus_callback.

The setup of (Weber et al., 2022): an n × n soybean field of 1 m cells (4 ha), colonised by 8000 adult female stink bugs over the first 10 days in its central 81 × 81 m, for 80 days. releases releases of per_ha female wasps, from day 4 and a week apart, go in mode (:strips or :points) spacing m apart in the central hectare; offspring = false (the source's main experiment) removes parasitised eggs at the wasps' emergence. The state at time t is the field at the start of day t, after that day's arrivals. Nothing lies beyond the field.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.telenomus_step! — Function
telenomus_step!(u, p, day, rng) -> u

The part of a day of (Weber et al., 2022) that reaches beyond a cell, done on the state u after the day's within-cell step, so that u becomes the field at the start of day day:

  1. Stink bugs move, cell by cell: each nymph older than dispersal_age and each adult moves along each axis by a distance drawn from nymph_steps or adult_steps, staying put on a draw of 0 and 0 and otherwise taking a free place in the cell drawn (drawing again if it is full or outside the field). A mature adult then lays an egg mass with probability p_oviposit in the first free place among the 3 × 3 cells around it, in random order.
  2. Wasps move, cell by cell in random order: each picks at random a cell within radius that holds unparasitised eggs, or any cell within radius when none does, and, from its second day, parasitises ⌊fecundity e^(-fecundity_decay (age - 1))⌋ eggs in its new cell and then in the 8 cells around it, in random order. Wasps then age a day.
  3. Colonists arrive (in the first colonisation_days days) in the central square, aged 1 to 13 days, and wasps are released on release days at telenomus_release_cells, filling a cell to 550 and spilling over into the 4 × 4 block (points) or the next row (strips).

Potato tuber moths and farmers

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_potato_moth_farmers — Function
build_potato_moth_farmers() -> CAModel

The potato tuber moth model of (Rebaudo et al., 2011), as in the authors' published NetLogo code, in steps of one moth generation on a lattice of 500 m cells. Each cell holds immature moths (immature, a real number), the origin of its moths (source: 0 none, 1 spread by flight from the first infestation, 2 and 3 brought by a farmer of the red or the blue team), the temperature the coming generation meets (temperature_now), and fixed layers of mean temperature (temperature, °C) and village population (population). The farmers stand on cells as bits of farmers, and those carrying moths as bits of carriers (see potato_moth_farmers_step!). Each generation, in every cell at once:

  1. immatures die of crude mortality 1 - exp(-(mort_dispersal + mort_predation) mort_time) and then of temperature (potato_moth_survival at temperature_now); half the survivors are gravid females, of which farmers kill control;
  2. an empty cell (no surviving immatures) takes the source of a neighbour with gravid females, chosen at random;
  3. a share diffusion of the gravid females leaves each cell, a quarter to each of its four neighbours; shares that would leave the lattice stay;
  4. gravid females, capped at capacity, give immatures by the logistic map capacity G / (G + (capacity - G) exp(-growth));
  5. the temperature the next generation meets is drawn from a normal distribution about temperature with standard deviation temperature_sd.

With neighbourhood = false, moths do not leave their cells (2 and 3 are skipped).

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_potato_moth_farmers — Function

The setup of the published model of (Rebaudo et al., 2011): the Simiatug valley (potato_moth_landscape), with 90 immature moths in the cell of Simiatug village, and farmers farmers (an even number, half in each team; 0 for none) placed at random, none carrying moths, for generations generations. Each farmer's pest control knowledge is drawn from seed:

  • knowledge = :uniform, as in the source's code: a whole number from 0 to 99;

  • or a vector giving each farmer's knowledge.

infection is :threshold (the source's code: a farmer buys infested potatoes if its knowledge is below threshold, 100 by default, so every farmer does) or :probability (with probability 1 - knowledge/100). The temperature the first generation meets is drawn from seed. Nothing lies beyond the valley.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.potato_moth_farmers_step! — Function
potato_moth_farmers_step!(u, p, rng) -> u

The farmers' part of a generation, applied to the state u in place after the moths' step: moves times, every farmer, in a random order, first

  • if it carries no moths and its cell has immatures, makes every farmer there whose knowledge is below threshold (infection = :threshold, the source's code) or, with infection = :probability, each with probability 1 - knowledge/100, a carrier;
  • if it carries moths and its cell has none, leaves inoculum immatures there, marked with its team;

and then every farmer, in a random order, travels to another village (a cell with a population). With probability p_nearest it follows the source's rule: it goes to the village j minimising w n pop_j d_j / population_scale, where w is its cell's population over population_scale, n its immatures over capacity and d_j the distance, ties broken at random (so a farmer standing where there are no moths, or outside a village, picks a village at random). Otherwise it picks one at random. Carriers stay carriers.

A Bt crop, two pests and their enemy on three layers

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_bt_crop_three_layer — Function
build_bt_crop_three_layer() -> CAModel

The three-layer model of (Yang et al., 2009), with the individuals of (He et al., 2002), one cycle a step. Layer M3 holds the plant A (plant, in units of its maximum), layer M2 the target pest B or the secondary pest C (pest: 0 empty, 1 B, 2 C) and layer M1 their natural enemy D (enemy). Every individual has an age, a hunger and a 32-bit Penna genome (pest_age, pest_hunger, pest_genome and the same for enemy). In each cycle:

  1. on M1 and M2 separately, the lattice is cut into pairs of contiguous cells (a random domino tiling: the Margolus blocks, each split into two horizontal or two vertical pairs); an individual next to an empty cell moves into it, or, in a birth cycle (age at least its maturity R, then every r cycles), leaves an offspring there with its genome and one more deleterious mutation;
  2. in every cell, an enemy above a pest eats it with probability P_DB (B) or P_DC (C), and its hunger falls by h_DB or h_DC (not below 0); a pest above at least d_B (or d_C) of plant eats that much with probability P_BA (or P_CA), and its hunger falls by h_BA (or h_CA); an individual that does not eat grows hungrier by U_B, U_C or U_D;
  3. individuals age by one; one dies when its hunger exceeds hmax_B, hmax_C or hmax_D, when the diseases its genome expresses up to its age reach T_B, T_C or T_D, or at max_age; the plant regrows by regrowth, up to its maximum.

Solve it with the Margolus() scheme on a periodic lattice with even sides.

problem_bt_crop_three_layer(; n=100, occupancy=0.5, cycles=1000, seed=0, pmap=(;),

The setup of (Yang et al., 2009) on an n × n torus (n even; the source uses 500 and 2000 cycles): the plant at its maximum everywhere, an enemy in each cell of M1 with probability occupancy, and a pest in each cell of M2 with probability occupancy, B or C with equal probability. Individuals start with clean genomes, no hunger and ages drawn uniformly below their maturity age (and the maximum age). Drawn from seed; parameters through pmap. Solve with solve(prob, Margolus()).
