Bio-geomorphology

Builders, problem constructors and helpers of the models whose category is "bio-geomorphology": landforms shaped by vegetation.

Coastal dunes

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_dune_formation — Function
build_dune_formation() -> CAModel

The beach-dune model of (Teixeira et al., 2023) (DuBeVeg). Each cell of 1 m holds an elevation in slabs of height h (topo), the cover of a pioneer and of a stabilizing plant (pioneer, stabilizer; their sum, at most 1, is the vegetation effectiveness ρ), the slabs gained or lost this year (balance), the elevation of the reference beach surface (zref), the probability that the sea has reset the cell this year (sea), and the elevation of a non-erodible surface (hard: a seawall or a promenade; none by default), below which neither wind, avalanches nor the sea remove sand and on which no plants grow until sand covers it. Rows run cross-shore, from the sea at the first column to the land at the last; the wind blows landward. The dynamics are dune_step!, a callback stored with the problem.

Beyond the source, speedup > 0 lets the topography steer the wind (dune_wind): the erosion probability scales with the shear stress along the wind, and flying slabs turn alongshore with the shear across it. The default, 0, is the source's wind, uniform and straight.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_dune_formation — Function
problem_dune_formation(; scenario=:wide, representation=:saltation, alongshore=100,
                       years=50, rate=4, landform=nothing, seed=0, pmap=(;),
                       kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The setup of (Teixeira et al., 2023) for its :narrow (100 m) or :wide (500 m) beach (see dune_profile), or its :prograding beach: the narrow beach on a domain extending rate × years m further out to sea, whose reference surface builds out by rate m a year (a berm at 2 m growing by rate m, the profile below it moving seaward, dune_landform), anchored at the land; the sea moves the beach towards it. With alongshore rows of 1 m, for years years of 50 aeolian iterations. representation = :saltation is the source's change (slabs of 0.02 m hopping 5 cells, trapped part-way by vegetation of at least 0.3); :default is DuBeVeg as it was (slabs of 0.1 m hopping 1 cell). Plants cover the foredune fully above 6 m and with a random cover between 4 and 6 m, pioneers seaward of each row's highest cell and stabilizers landward of it (the source does not say which). Water levels come from dune_water_levels, drawn from seed.

Three more scenarios run landforms the source does not (see dune_landform): :double_ridge, the wide beach with an embryo dune seaward of the foredune; :seawall, the narrow beach ending at a seawall and promenade, which neither wind, avalanches nor the sea can erode (the hard layer); and :blowout, the narrow beach with the foredune breached in the middle fifth of the rows.

landform, a ProfileSegment or a vector of them (one for each row, all of the same length), replaces the scenario's profile: its reference surface is reference_profile's, pieces of kind :seawall or :promenade are non-erodible, and the beach gradient for wave run-up is that of its :beach pieces (rise over length) unless pmap gives slope.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.dune_step! — Function
dune_step!(u, p, t, rng) -> Int

One aeolian iteration t of (Teixeira et al., 2023), applied to the state u in place: the shadow zones and the probabilities of erosion and deposition are fixed from the state at its start; every cell above the groundwater surface (G times the reference surface) and above sea level, outside the shadow, gives up a slab with probability (1 - ρ) pe0; each slab moves landward a cell at a time, landing at the end of every hop of L cells with probability ρ (1 - pd0) + pd0 (1 in the shadow) and, part-way through a hop, with the same probability where ρ ≥ rho_star (never otherwise); slabs leaving the land side are lost. Avalanches then restore the angles of repose. Every hydro_every iterations the sea acts with the next water level of water_levels plus wave run-up (dune_runup): seaward of the first cell above that level, each cell is reset to the reference surface with probability (1 - Rv ρ)(wave + Pinun) (at most 1), where the wave strength (WE - Wdiss D) × wave_energy × level (the level in slabs, as in DuBeVeg's code), no less than wave_min, falls with D, the sum of the inverse water depths (m, at least depth_min) of the cells crossed. Avalanches follow; every iterations iterations the plants grow, and their cover is then multiplied by the chance that the sea did not reset the cell during the year, as in DuBeVeg, and, if references is not empty, the reference surface becomes that of the year just ended (a prograding beach). Returns the slabs that crossed the dune toe (the first cell above 3 m) in all rows.

With speedup > 0 (beyond the source), the shear stress of dune_wind is computed from the state at the start of the iteration: the erosion probability is multiplied by the shear along the wind (and capped at 1), and at each cell a flying slab moves one row alongshore, towards the side the shear across the wind points to, with probability |across| / along (at most 1; never off the domain).

EcologicalCellularAutomata.dune_runup — Function
dune_runup(η, slope) -> Float64

The wave run-up (m) above a water level η (m) on a beach of gradient slope, by Stockdon et al.'s relation as DuBeVeg applies it, with the wave height and length correlated with the water level: H = max(0, 2.931 η - 2.637), L = max(0, 46.74 η - 30.59).

EcologicalCellularAutomata.dune_landform — Function
dune_landform(; form=:foredune, beach=500, offshore=60, toe=2.5, berm=0, berm_level=2,
              embryo=40, embryo_crest=7, slack=160, wall=7.5, breach=5) -> ProfileSegment

A cross-shore profile as joined pieces (ProfileSegment), from the sea to the land, offshore + beach + 120 m long. With form = :foredune, the source's: a Dean shoreface over offshore m (shoreface_segment), a beach rising linearly from 0 at the shoreline to toe m at the foot of the dune over beach m (2.5 m, as the source's figure shows; its text says 3 m), a foredune ridge 100 m wide rising to 15 m (a smooth bump, its shape read from the source's figure; ridge_segment), and a 5 m plateau behind it. With berm > 0 the beach is cut where it reaches berm_level m and a level berm of berm m is inserted there: the beach of the source's prograding scenario after it has built out by berm m. Berms add: the landform with berm = a + b is the one with berm = a with b m more of berm joined in.

Other forms, which the source does not run, are composed of the same pieces (and have the same length):

  • :double_ridge: an embryo dune embryo m wide rising to embryo_crest m stands on the beach, its land side slack m seaward of the foredune, with a level dune slack (kind :slack) at toe m between them.
  • :seawall: the beach ends at a wall (kind :seawall, 2 m, rising from toe to wall m) with a level promenade (kind :promenade) behind it, in place of the dune and plateau.
  • :breach: the foredune is lowered to a trough rising from toe to breach m before falling to the plateau: a blowout through the ridge, for the rows it cuts.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.dune_water_levels — Function
dune_water_levels(rng; years=50, level=1.43, surge=0.24) -> Vector{Float64}

A synthetic series of the highest water level (m above mean sea level) in each spring-neap cycle, 25 a year, standing in for the source's observed levels at Scheveningen: level m plus a Gumbel-distributed surge of scale surge m. The defaults are fitted to the series the source plots (its Fig. 5d, read from the figure): a median of about 1.5 m, a tenth of the cycles above 2 m and a highest level of about 3.2 m in 50 years.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.dune_wind — Function
dune_wind(u, p) -> NamedTuple

The surface shear stress of the wind over the state u, relative to its value over a flat bed, by the linear theory of turbulent flow over low hills (Jackson & Hunt; Weng et al.) in the form of (Kroy et al., 2002) along the wind and (Schwämmle and Herrmann, 2005) across it: with ĥ the Fourier transform of the height above the reference surface zref (m) and kx, ky the wave numbers along and across the wind (per m),

along  = 1 + F⁻¹{A kx (kx + i B |kx|) / |k| ĥ}
across =     F⁻¹{A kx ky / |k| ĥ}

where A = p.speedup and B = p.upwind_shift. The wind speeds up over crests and windward slopes, most just upwind of a crest, slows at the foot of a slope, and turns towards the normal of a ridge it crosses at an angle: into a gap through a ridge, out of it beyond. In the lee the theory is applied to the envelope of the topography and the shadow lines (15° by default, p.shadow), as in (Kroy et al., 2002). The domain is mirrored alongshore and, cross-shore, extended by a smooth return from the land edge to the sea edge, so the transform sees no jumps. Returns along and across (rows by columns; rows run alongshore, increasing across points to higher rows).

Landform profiles

Cross-shore profiles built by joining pieces from the sea to the land; two pieces join where the first ends at the elevation where the second starts.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.ProfileSegment — Type
ProfileSegment(kind, z0, z1, heights)

A piece of a cross-shore profile: the elevations (m) of consecutive 1 m cells, from the sea to the land, heights, starting from elevation z0 (the seaward edge) and ending at z1 (the landward edge, the last cell's elevation for the built-in pieces). kind names the piece (:shoreface, :beach, :berm, :dune, :hinterland or any other Symbol), which reference_profile reads. Pieces are joined with *, in order from the sea, when the first ends where the second starts; a profile is itself a ProfileSegment (of kind :profile) that remembers its pieces.

Built-in pieces: shoreface_segment, slope_segment, flat_segment and ridge_segment.

source
Base.:* — Method
a * b -> ProfileSegment

Join two profile pieces, a on the seaward side of b. The elevation where a ends must be the elevation where b starts (to 1e-9 m); otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown. Joining is associative, and an empty piece at an elevation joins with no effect.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.flat_segment — Function

A level stretch at z m over length m: a berm, a plateau behind a dune (kind=:hinterland), or, with length = 0, the empty piece at z. Level pieces at one elevation join into a longer one: flat_segment(a, z) * flat_segment(b, z) has the heights of flat_segment(a + b, z).

EcologicalCellularAutomata.reference_profile — Function
reference_profile(profile) -> ProfileSegment

The surface the sea works towards: the profile with its dunes and hinterland removed, every piece of kind :dune or :hinterland replaced by a level piece at the elevation of the foot of the first dune. It works piece by piece and keeps the joins: the reference of a * b is the reference of a joined to that of b when both lie seaward of the dune or both landward.
