Pine wilt disease

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_pine_wilt_casim — Function
build_pine_wilt_casim(; weights=pine_wilt_weights(1), incubation=false) -> CAModel

The pine wilt disease automaton of (Huang et al., 2012), in yearly steps. Each cell is a 10 m pixel: non-forest (0), other forest (1), healthy pine (2), latent (3) or symptomatic (4) infected pine, or dead pine (5); the layer years counts years in the latent or symptomatic state.

  • A healthy pine is infected with probability min(1, Σₙ wₙ Cₙ / (8n)), where Cₙ is the number of latent or symptomatic pines in the square ring at distance n (8n cells) and weights gives wₙ for rings 1:length(weights). With incubation = true a new infection is latent with probability p_latent and symptomatic otherwise; without, it is symptomatic.
  • A latent pine stays latent with probability p_stay, for at most max_latent years, and otherwise shows symptoms.
  • A symptomatic pine is felled with probability α a year and becomes other forest; otherwise it dies after max_symptomatic years.

Other cells do not change. Nothing lies beyond a landscape: edges reflect.

A spruce forest and bark beetles

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_bark_beetle_forest — Function
build_bark_beetle_forest() -> CAModel

The spruce forest model of (Königswieser and Jahn, 2020), in yearly steps. Each cell of 1 m² holds at most one tree (tree), with an age in years (age), a dry wood mass in kg (mass), a number of bark beetles (beetles), a fate for the coming year (fate: 0 standing, 1 felled, 2 killed by beetles) and a fixed flag for contaminated ground (contaminated). Each year, in every cell:

  • a tree felled or killed by beetles the year before is removed, with its beetles;
  • a tree ages a year and gains growth_young kg a year until mature_age, then growth_old kg until final_age;
  • it dies with probability mort_sapling up to sapling_age, mort_adult up to old_age, and mort_old beyond, and at once on contaminated ground past contaminated_age.

In a drought year (droughts, each lasting drought_length years) growth is multiplied by drought_growth, mortality by drought_mortality and the chance of spreading by drought_spread.

Spreading, strip harvest, infestation and the beetles' spread are bark_beetle_step!, a callback stored with the problem.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_bark_beetle_forest — Function
problem_bark_beetle_forest(; n=300, years=300, contamination=false, harvest=false,
                           droughts=(), infestation=nothing, seed=0, pmap=(;),
                           kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The setup of (Königswieser and Jahn, 2020): an n × n forest of 1 m cells (9 ha for the default), starting from 20 trees aged 10 (and 280 kg) in distinct cells within 20 m of the centre, placed at random from seed, for years years. Each scenario is off unless given:

  • contamination = true contaminates the rows 90 to 120 and columns 120 to 180;
  • harvest = true fells the strips from year 101 to year 250;
  • droughts lists the years after which a drought of 10 years starts, such as (50, 250);
  • infestation is the year 2000 beetles are brought into the rows and columns 100 to 120.

The state at time t is the forest at the end of year t. Nothing lies beyond the forest.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.bark_beetle_step! — Function
bark_beetle_step!(u, p, year, rng) -> u

The part of the forest's year that depends on the order of events, applied to the state u in place after the rule's step, in this order:

  1. With harvest, in the years after harvest_start for harvest_years years, the trees older than harvest_age in one of strips strips of rows, in turn, are felled, with their beetles.
  2. In year infestation, infest_draws trees, drawn with replacement from those standing (not felled) in the rows infest_rows and columns infest_cols, each gain infest_beetles beetles. If no tree stands there, none do.
  3. beetle_spreads times, all at once, every tree with beetles multiplies them by beetle_growth, and the same number is shared equally among the other standing trees in the square of half-width beetle_radius around it; with whole_beetles each share is rounded down.
  4. Every tree with beetle_kill beetles or more is killed, and its beetles with it.
  5. In years divisible by spread_interval, the trees standing at that moment, those felled or killed this year included, are visited in column order. A tree with n other trees in the square of half-width neighbour_radius around it spreads with probability spread_probabilities[k], for the first class k with n ≤ spread_limits[k] (none beyond the last class). Its sapling, of age 0, lands in a random cell of the square of half-width sapling_radius, and takes root only if no tree stands within distance spacing of it and at most site_limit trees stand in the square of half-width neighbour_radius around it, saplings planted earlier in the same year included.

Trees felled or killed are removed at the start of the next year.

Mountain pine beetles across scales

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_mpb_multiscale — Function
build_mpb_multiscale() -> CAModel

The microscale of the mountain pine beetle model of (Liu and Estep, 2025): each cell's normalised beetle density (density, from 0 to 1) becomes the mean, over replicates independent runs, of a birth-death process started from density × capacity beetles and run for micro_steps steps of length 1/micro_steps, divided by capacity. In each step a population of x gains a beetle with probability b x (1 - x/capacity) / micro_steps, loses one with probability d x / micro_steps, and is set to 0 when below 1 at the start of a step, as in the source's code. The rates b and d are birth and death with schedule = :constant; with :periodic, the birth rate is multiplied by 1 + amplitude sin(2π t / period); with :outbreak, seasonal rates switch from endemic to epidemic amplitudes after step switch (see the source's code). With integer_population, the starting number is rounded, as the source's article defines it. Blocked cells (blocked) hold no beetles.

The macroscale dispersal is mpb_disperse!, a callback stored with the problem.

The setup of (Liu and Estep, 2025): an n × n grid of hexagons with absorbing edges, starting from pattern (a name for mpb_pattern, or a matrix of densities), for steps steps. blocked is an optional n × n Boolean matrix of barrier cells. The defaults are the article's Table 2: birth = 1, death = 0.2, gamma1 = gamma2 = 0.8, phi = rho = 1/3, capacity = 100, micro_steps = 100, replicates = 20.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.mpb_disperse! — Function
mpb_disperse!(u, p, rng) -> u

The macroscale dispersal of (Liu and Estep, 2025), applied to the state u in place after the microscale step. Affected cells (density above 0) form interaction neighbourhoods: with linkage = 0 all of them form one, as in the source's code; otherwise those linked by chains of affected cells at most linkage steps apart. Each neighbourhood, with all the immediate neighbours of its cells (including auxiliary cells beyond the lattice, which can receive but not keep beetles), disperses independently:

  1. each cell is drawn into the dispersal with probability (w^alpha d / (1 + w^alpha))^beta, where w counts its affected neighbours (plus 1 if it is affected itself) and d is the largest absolute difference between its density and its neighbours'; with wind, the probability of cells below the neighbourhood's mean density and upwind of its centre is multiplied by wind_factor;
  2. among the k cells drawn, those at or above their mean density π̄ contribute gamma1 (π - π̄), pooled into π̃;
  3. the others receive gamma2 π̃ / k and keep their beetles with probability phi + rho atan(xi π) at their new density, or lose them all;
  4. contributing cells become π - gamma1 (π - π̄) + π̃ / k, as in the source's code, or π - gamma1 (π - π̄) + gamma2 π̃ / k with gamma2_contributors, as its article writes it.

Blocked cells take no part and are no one's neighbours.

Emerald ash borer

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_emerald_ash_borer — Function
build_emerald_ash_borer() -> CAModel

The emerald ash borer model of (Mercader et al., 2011), in yearly steps. Each cell holds ash phloem (m²), the dispersed adults (females) that will lay there this year, the larvae laid last year that emerge next year (larvae1), the larvae that take two years, laid in odd and in even years (larvae2_odd, larvae2_even), and whether it lies in the border around the landscape (border), where dispersing beetles are lost, and its phloem at the start (phloem0). Each year, in every cell at once:

  1. the adults lay min(r0 adults, phloem / C) larvae, which eat C m² of phloem each;
  2. a share p2 = -0.194 ln(x) + 0.986 (kept within 0 and 1) of the larvae take two years to develop, where x is the larvae per m² of the phloem left; the rest emerge next year.

The emergence and dispersal of adults are eab_disperse!, a callback stored with the problem.

source

problem_emerald_ash_borer(; environment=:homogeneous, n=205, founders=100,
                          behaviour=:avoidance, years=15, seed=0, pmap=(;),
                          kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The setup of (Mercader et al., 2011): an environment of phloem (a name for eab_environment, drawn from seed, or a matrix) of n × n cells of 50 m, with founders adults at its centre that emerge and disperse in the first year, for years years. As in the source's code, the landscape is surrounded by a border of 2 cells of 200 m² of phloem where dispersing beetles are lost. The model is otherwise deterministic. The state at time t is the landscape after the larvae of year t have fed, with the adults that will lay in year t + 1 already dispersed.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.eab_disperse! — Function
eab_disperse!(u, p, t; attraction=nothing) -> u

The emergence and dispersal of adults of (Mercader et al., 2011), applied to the state u in place after year t, for year t + 1. The larvae of one year (larvae1) and the two-year larvae of the matching parity emerge, and the adults from each cell spread over the cells within window cells (clipped at the edge of the lattice) in proportion to exp(-b cell (D + y)), normalised source by source, where D is the distance between cell centres in cells (self_distance for the cell itself) and y an extra distance to the receiving cell:

  • :standard: none;
  • :avoidance: exit for cells without phloem (at most bare m²);
  • :resource_bias (inferred from the source's description): as avoidance, and D shortened by bias for cells that started with phloem at or above the upper quartile over the landscape and lengthened by bias for those at or below the lower quartile, unless the two quartiles are equal (so a uniform landscape has no bias).

If attraction is given (a matrix the size of the lattice), each receiving cell's weight is also multiplied by it before normalising. Adults landing in border cells are lost, and so are those of a cell holding no more than min_source (0 by default).

Two parameters reproduce the source's code exactly. In the code a cell is without phloem when its phloem is at most 0, but a cell whose phloem has just been eaten keeps a round-off error of either sign, so which eaten cells count as bare depends on the order of arithmetic; bare (default 0, as the code) sets the threshold, and a small one (such as 1e-9) makes every eaten cell count. With transposed = true (square lattices only) the adults of cell (i, j) spread from cell (j, i), as the code's indexing does; this changes nothing on landscapes symmetric about the diagonal.

Managing emerald ash borer outliers

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_eab_management — Function
build_eab_management() -> CAModel

The management model of (Mercader et al., 2011), the emerald ash borer model of (Mercader et al., 2011) (see build_emerald_ash_borer) with a treatment applied to the cells within a radius of the origin, as the source's code computes it. Besides the base model's layers, each cell holds whether it is treated and its phloem in large trees (m²), which alone can be treated. Each year t, in every cell at once, the adults lay N = min(r0 adults, phloem / C) larvae; from the year start on,

  1. under girdling, a treated cell girdles a tree of girdle_area m² (no more than its large-tree phloem), which takes a share y g / (y g + phloem - g) of the larvae, where y = max(girdle_a (x + girdle_shift)^girdle_b, girdle_min) is the ratio of the density of larvae on girdled to other trees at the density x = N / phloem (or girdle_a with attraction_decline = false); the tree is removed with them;
  2. under insecticide, the larvae laid in a treated cell's large-tree phloem die (x times its area);
  3. the larvae left eat C m² of phloem each, the insecticide-protected share excepted, as in the source's code, which reduces the feeding by the unprotected share a second time;
  4. under ash reduction, in the year start only, a treated cell's remaining large-tree phloem is removed after the larvae have fed;

and the larvae take one or two years to develop, as in the base model. The large-tree phloem loses its share of what is eaten. The stress bias, emergence and dispersal are eab_manage!, a callback stored with the problem.

The setup of (Mercader et al., 2011): a landscape of n × n cells of 50 m with phloem m² of ash phloem each (or a matrix of it), a share of it in large trees (none in a cell where that is at most large_min m²), and founders adults at the origin, the cell (n ÷ 2 + 1, n ÷ 2 + 1), which emerge and disperse in the first year, for years years, with the treatment applied to the cells within radius m of the origin from year start (0 is the founders' first year). As in the source's code, there is no border: dispersal is normalised within the landscape. The model is deterministic.
