Grapevine leafroll

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_grapevine_leafroll — Function
build_grapevine_leafroll(; undetectable=:duration) -> CAModel

The grapevine leafroll model of (Atallah et al., 2015), in monthly steps. Each cell is a vine with an infection state (state: 0 healthy, 1 exposed-undetectable, 2 exposed-detectable, 3 infective-moderate, 4 infective-high), an age in months (age), a clock (clock: the months left undetectable, or the months spent in an infective state), and flags set by the management callback (rogued, tested). Columns of the lattice are trellis rows. Each month, synchronously:

  • a healthy vine is infected with probability 1 - exp(-(nα α + nβ β)), where nα and nβ count the infective (3 or 4) neighbours along its trellis row and in the neighbouring rows;
  • with undetectable = :duration a new infection stays undetectable for a triangular number of months (eu_min, eu_mode, eu_max, rounded up); with :cdf, as the source writes it, an undetectable vine becomes detectable each month with probability F(x), the triangular distribution function at the months x it has been undetectable;
  • an exposed-detectable vine becomes infective-moderate with probability 1 - exp(-1/L), where L is L_young, L_mature or L_old by age (under 60 months, under 240, older);
  • an infective-moderate vine becomes infective-high with probability 1 - exp(-1/inf_period);
  • every vine ages by a month.

Management is grapevine_strategy_callback.

The initial infections are exposed-undetectable (initial = :Eu, as the source says "exposed"), exposed-detectable (:Ed) or infective-moderate (:Im). Nothing lies beyond the vineyard.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.grapevine_manage! — Function
grapevine_manage!(u, strategy; delay=0, maturity=36, protect_replants=true)
    -> (rogued, tested)

Apply a management strategy of (Atallah et al., 2015) to the vineyard state u in place, and flag the vines rogued and tested. Every vine meeting the trigger (:I, infective; :Im, infective-moderate; :Ih, infective-high; with an age class Y, M or O appended, only vines of that class) is rogued and replaced by a healthy vine of age 0. A spatial strategy (:ImNS, :ImNSEW, :ImNS2EW, :ImNS2EW2, and their age-class forms such as :ImM_NSEW) also tests the listed neighbours of each triggering vine that are not infective, once each, and rogues those found exposed-detectable. With protect_replants, vines younger than maturity months are never rogued. A vine triggers only once it has been infective for delay months, so that with delay = 1 it infects its neighbours for a month before it is found. Returns the numbers of vines rogued and tested.

Western X disease in a cherry orchard

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_western_x_orchard — Function
build_western_x_orchard() -> CAModel

The Western X disease model of (Ghorbani et al., 2026), in monthly steps. Each cell is a cherry tree with a disease state (0 an empty cell where a tree was cut, 1 unvisited, 2 visited by an infective leafhopper, 3 infected, 4 visible, 5 high), an age in months, a clock of months in its state, and flags set by the management callback (cut, replanted, tested). Each month, in every cell at once:

  • an unvisited tree is inoculated with probability alpha + beta k, where k counts the visible and high trees among its eight neighbours; with rates = :annual this is a yearly probability, converted to a monthly one;
  • a visited, infected or visible tree that has spent a whole number of years in its state moves to the next with probability progression;
  • every tree ages a month.

Management is western_x_manage!; the economics are western_x_economics.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_western_x_orchard — Function
problem_western_x_orchard(; strategy=:none, rows=20, cols=30, initial=(0.02, 0.08),
                          disease=true, years=25, seed=0, pmap=(;), kwargs...)
    -> CAProblem

The setup of (Ghorbani et al., 2026): a newly planted orchard of rows × cols trees (an acre), of which a share drawn uniformly from initial (a range, or a number), chosen at random from seed, start visited, for years years in monthly steps, under a management strategy (see western_x_manage!; :none for none). With disease = false, no tree is ever inoculated (the source's disease-free scenario). Nothing lies beyond the orchard. Management decides every other year, as in the source: from month 24 for threshold strategies and from month 48 for testing strategies, unless pmap says otherwise.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.western_x_manage! — Function
western_x_manage!(u, p, month, rng) -> u

The fixed management strategies of (Ghorbani et al., 2026), applied to the state u in place after the disease's step, in decision months (month at or after first_decision, every interval months). Visual scouting finds every visible and high tree.

  • :threshold_cut and :threshold_replant cut every symptomatic tree once at least threshold of the cells hold one (and replant it, as an unvisited tree of age 0).
  • :test_cut and :test_replant also test a share test_share of the trees within Moore radius radius of each symptomatic tree that are not symptomatic themselves; a test detects a visited or infected tree with probability sensitivity and never errs on an unvisited one. Symptomatic and detected trees are cut (and replanted).

The trees cut, replanted and tested are flagged for the month.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.western_x_economics — Function
western_x_economics(sol; price=2.35, discount=0.05, no_harvest=0.25, cut_cost=33,
                    plant_cost=14.3, test_cost=300, scale_costs=false) -> NamedTuple

The economics of a run saved every month, per orchard, as in (Ghorbani et al., 2026): each tree yields y(age) lb a month and costs c(age) dollars a month; the yield is multiplied by 1 - loss, with losses 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 in the visited, infected, visible and high states, and so is the cost with scale_costs = true, as the source's equation writes it; an empty cell yields and costs nothing. A month in which at least no_harvest of the cells hold symptomatic trees brings no revenue (the source's growers do not harvest a badly diseased block). Each tree cut costs cut_cost, each replanted plant_cost more, each tested test_cost. Values are discounted at discount a year. Returns the yearly yield (lb) and net revenue (net, undiscounted), and the net present value npv.

Sudden oak death in California

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_sudden_oak_death — Function
build_sudden_oak_death(; short=sod_kernel(20.57; radius=4),
                       long=sod_kernel(9504; radius=99), method=:fft) -> CAModel

The sudden oak death model of (Meentemeyer et al., 2011), in weekly steps. Each cell holds S susceptible and I infected host units (at most nmax together), a forest type (forest: 0 none, 1 redwood-tanoak, 2 mixed evergreen) and whether it releases spores this week (sporulating). In week t, with weather index w_t (moisture times temperature suitability, from weather), every infected unit in a sporulating cell releases a Poisson number of spore units at rate beta w_t; each travels by the short-range kernel short with probability gamma and by the long-range kernel long otherwise (with the long-range share multiplied by long_factor from week management_week on); where it lands, in a cell whose forest is in season, it infects one of the S susceptible units with probability w_t S / nmax. New infections in a cell are therefore Poisson, capped at S. Redwood-tanoak forest takes part from January to September and mixed evergreen from February to September. method = :fft computes the long-range field by fast Fourier transform.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_sudden_oak_death — Function
problem_sudden_oak_death(; n=100, weather=:random, years=1990:2030, observed=1990:2008,
                         sites=3, wetness=0.5, management=nothing, seed=0, pmap=(;),
                         kwargs...)
    -> CAProblem

The setup of (Meentemeyer et al., 2011) on a synthetic landscape of n × n cells of 250 m: host units from a random cluster landscape (half the cells hold none), redwood-tanoak forest on about a third of the host cells and mixed evergreen on the rest, and sites host cells near the centre (within n / 8 cells of it, or the nearest host cells if there are too few there) each with one infected unit at the start of years. The weather of the observed years is drawn by sod_weather with wetness; later years repeat observed years at random (weather = :random), from the better half by weather index (:favourable) or from the worse half (:unfavourable), as in the source. management = (year, factor) multiplies the long-range share of dispersal by factor from that year on. All is drawn from seed. The landscape lies in the corner of a torus twice its size whose other cells are masked, so spores leaving it are lost; the state at time t is the end of week t.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.sod_kernel — Function
sod_kernel(scale; radius, cell=250, samples=16) -> Matrix{Float64}

The probability that a spore released at the centre of a cell lands in each cell within radius cells, for a Cauchy dispersal kernel of scale metres on cells of side cell metres: the spore travels in a uniform direction a distance from the half-Cauchy distribution 2 / (π scale (1 + (d/scale)²)), so that the kernel (1 + (d/scale)²)^-1 of (Meentemeyer et al., 2011) is the density per unit distance. The centre cell is integrated exactly; the others by the midpoint rule on samples × samples points. Spores landing beyond the radius are lost, so the probabilities sum to less than one.

The source's suitability of a week of mean temperature x °C for sporulation, fitted to Davidson et al. (2005): -0.066 + 0.056 x - 0.0036 (x - 15)² - 0.0003 (x - 15)³, at least 0.

Pitch canker in Europe

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_pitch_canker — Function
build_pitch_canker(; spreading_years=5, spore_max=1300, cell=1000) -> CAModel

The pitch canker model of (Möykkynen et al., 2015), in yearly steps on square cells of side cell metres. Each cell holds its healthy host trees (hosts), its host coverage (coverage, 0 to 1), the ecoclimatic index of the current and the future climate (ei_now, ei_future, 0 to 1), the trees infected this year (new), those infected 1 to spreading_years years ago (i1, i2, ...), which spread the fungus, the dead trees (dead) and the year of its first infection (infected_year, 0 for none). With N_max the most host trees in a cell (trees_per_ha times the cell's area in hectares) and EI the index in year y, interpolated linearly from ei_now in the first year (first_year) to ei_future in climate_year, each year, in every cell at once:

  1. the infected trees age: this year's infections join those spreading, and those infected spreading_years years ago die;
  2. spores from every cell whose midpoint lies within spore_max metres (the cell itself included) give a density Σ (N_spreading / N_max) exp(-d / spore_mean) over those cells at distance d (Eqs. 2 and 3), and N_healthy EI density healthy trees are infected (Eq. 4): its whole part, and one more with probability its fractional part, at most every healthy tree.

Arrivals of infected seedling lots and the flights of insects are pitch_canker_spread!, a callback stored with the problem.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_pitch_canker — Function
problem_pitch_canker(; dims=(600, 200), years=20, spreading_years=5, coverage=nothing, ei=nothing,

The setup of (Möykkynen et al., 2015) on cells of 1 km: years yearly steps from a landscape with no infection, the fungus arriving through entries (see pitch_canker_spread!; by default pitch_canker_entries for the landscape's rows), with the source's parameters unless pmap overrides them. The host coverage and the current index ei are those of pitch_canker_landscape of size dims drawn from seed unless given (as matrices of one size); ei_future is the index of the future climate (by default the current one). Each cell starts with round(coverage N_max) healthy trees. The lattice is a Grid with a constant zero boundary, so spores, lots and insects leaving it are lost. spreading_years and pmap.spore_max and pmap.cell set the model's cohorts and reach (see build_pitch_canker); the callback is stored with the problem.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.pitch_canker_spread! — Function
pitch_canker_spread!(u, p, t, rng) -> u

The arrivals of infected seedling lots and the flights of infected insects of (Möykkynen et al., 2015) in year t, applied to the state u in place after the year's spores (see build_pitch_canker). Each infection takes one healthy tree of the cell where it lands with probability (N_healthy / N_max) EI (Eq. 1), lowering N_healthy at once; cells are squares of side cell metres, and points outside the lattice infect nothing.

  1. Every entry point (i, j, first, last, lots) in entries with first ≤ t ≤ last sends lots seedling lots from the midpoint of cell (i, j), each a uniform distance up to transport_max in a uniform direction, accepted with probability the destination cell's host coverage and drawn again otherwise (given up after transport_tries draws). Each lot sends dispatches spore dispatches from where it is planted, each a negative exponential distance of mean spore_mean (drawn again above spore_max) in a uniform direction, which infect where they land.
  2. The trees spreading this year (cohorts i1 to the last) of each cell send insects_per_tree infected insects each (the total's whole part, and one more with probability its fractional part) from the cell's midpoint. Each insect flies a distance of mean insect_mean, negative exponential or Weibull of shape insect_shape (insect_distribution), drawn again above insect_max, in a uniform direction, and tries to infect where it lands; if it fails it flies again from the tree, insect_tries times in all.

Lots are taken in the order of entries and insects cell by cell in column order, all drawing from rng. With insect_method = :each every insect is flown as above. With :pooled (the default, far faster when many trees spread) a cell's insects are drawn together: given the healthy trees when they set out, an insect infects in the cell at offset o, on one of its tries, with probability K(o) q(o) (1 - (1 - s)^T) / s, where K(o) is the probability that a flight from the midpoint lands there, q(o) = (N_healthy / N_max) EI there, s = Σ K q and T = insect_tries; the numbers infecting each cell are a multinomial draw over those probabilities, at most the cell's healthy trees. The two agree in distribution as long as one cell's insects take few of any cell's healthy trees; without a maximum flight (insect_max = Inf) the pooled draw loses the flights longer than the distance that fewer than one in a million exceed.

Bahia bark scaling of citrus

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_citrus_bark_scaling — Function
build_citrus_bark_scaling() -> CAModel

The spatial SEI model of Bahia bark scaling of citrus of (Cunniffe et al., 2014), fitted there to the grove of (Laranjeira et al., 2006). Each cell is a tree on a GraphSpace linking every tree with every other: susceptible (0), infected but not yet symptomatic (1), or symptomatic and infectious (2). A step is a month; in the step from month t:

  • a susceptible tree is infected with probability 1 - exp(-λ a(t)), where λ = β Σ exp(-d / α) / (2π α²) sums over the symptomatic trees at distances d (the covariate distance holds each tree's distances to its neighbours, in the order of the graph), and a(t) = clamp(t + 1 - δ, 0, 1) is the share of the month after the trees reach epidemiological maturity at month δ;
  • an infected tree becomes symptomatic with probability 1 - exp(-ρ).

It is a ProbabilisticRule, so path_logpdf gives the exact likelihood of a path observed every month, and citrus_loglik gives the same quickly from the months of infection and symptoms.

Flavescence dorée among vines

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_flavescence_doree — Function
build_flavescence_doree(; kernel=:power6, α=5.0, cultivar_effect=1.0, radius=60.0,
                        resolution=0.5) -> CAModel

The yearly SIR with roguing of (Adrakey et al., 2023) on a raster of resolution m. Layers: vine (0 no vine, 1 healthy, 2 infected and cryptic, 3 symptomatic and infectious, 4 removed) and cultivar (1 Merlot, 2 Cabernet Sauvignon). In each year's step a healthy vine is removed for other reasons with probability q, and otherwise infected with probability 1 - exp(-(ε + β Z field)); an infected vine becomes symptomatic, and a symptomatic one is rogued. A step is a season: a vine infected in the season before becomes symptomatic, and infects others, in this one (the vector acquires and transmits within a season), and is rogued in the winter after it. So the field pressure sums, over the vines infected the season before (state 2, symptomatic this season) within radius m, the kernel's weight (fd_kernel with scale α), times cultivar_effect for a Cabernet Sauvignon source (the source's cultivar effect is 1.42); Z (a parameter, set by problem_flavescence_doree) is the sum of the kernel's weights over the raster divided by α², which undoes the core's normalisation, so that the force of infection is ε + β Σ c_j K(r_ij; α) / α² as in the source. α and the kernel are fixed when the model is built, since the field's weights depend on them.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.FDHistory — Type
FDHistory

A history of the vines for the yearly model, by the years of the steps in which things happen: infected[i] (the vine is infected in the step from that year, symptomatic two years on and rogued after; 0 for never), removed[i] (removed for another reason in the step from that year; 0 for never), open_end[i] (the last step is summed over: the vine was healthy at the last survey, so susceptible or newly infected), and observed_to[i] (the last year whose step is observed; later steps are censored, as for a plot uprooted after a survey).

fd_survey_fit(prob) -> (fit, history)

The fitting data for the survey of (Adrakey et al., 2023) on the raster, kernel, radius and cultivar effect of prob (problem_flavescence_doree) (steps from prob.p.first_year to 2019): vines symptomatic in 2018 or 2019 were infected two years before; vines absent from a Merlot plot in 2019 were removed for other reasons in 2018; the Cabernet Sauvignon plot was uprooted after the 2018 survey, so its vines' last step is censored; vines healthy in 2019 have an open last step; and each vine absent in 2018 is latent, either rogued after showing symptoms in 2015, 2016 or 2017, or removed for another reason in a year before 2018 (its candidates). EcologicalCellularAutomata.fd_loglik — Function
fd_loglik(θ, z::FDHistory, fit::FDFit) -> Float64

The log-probability of the history z under the yearly model with parameters θ (ε, β, q, α): over every vine and every observed step in which it is healthy, log q if it is then removed for another reason, otherwise log(1 - q) plus -λ (it stays healthy) or log(1 - exp(-λ)) (it is infected), with λ = ε + β Σ c_j K(r_ij; α) / α² over the vines symptomatic, and infectious, in that season (infected in the step before); a vine healthy at the last survey contributes log(1 - q) for the last step. With every step observed and no open ends it is the core's path_logpdf of the corresponding path.
