Rhizoctonia solani: primary and secondary infection

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_rhizoctonia_contact — Function
build_rhizoctonia_contact(; secondary=:per_neighbour) -> CAModel

The primary and secondary infection model of (Kleczkowski et al., 1997), for damping-off of radish by Rhizoctonia solani in microcosms. Each cell is a plant, susceptible (0), latent (1) or infectious (2). A step is dt days of absolute time τ_p + t * dt. In each step, synchronously:

  • a susceptible plant becomes latent with probability 1 - exp(-R dt), where R is the sum of the primary rate C_p (1 + η_p x) exp(-σ_p x) exp(-d_p t) over the inoculum units in its square, at distances x, and the secondary rate C_s exp(-σ_s x_d²) exp(-d_s t) times the number of infectious neighbours, with C_p = a_p d_p exp(d_p τ_p) and C_s = a_s d_s exp(d_s τ_s);
  • a latent plant becomes infectious with probability 1 - exp(-g dt).

The neighbours are the von Neumann neighbours, with none beyond the edges. The primary part of R reads the covariate pathozone, the sum of (1 + η_p x) exp(-σ_p x) over the units in each plant's square, which rhizoctonia_pathozone computes. With secondary = :any the secondary rate is counted once if any neighbour is infectious, the literal reading of the source's rule, instead of once per infectious neighbour.

Viral fronts: a multicomponent Domany–Kinzel automaton

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_domany_kinzel_viral — Function
build_domany_kinzel_viral(; R=6) -> CAModel

The multicomponent Domany–Kinzel automaton of (Capitán et al., 2011), for the front of a viral infection in host tissue: a one-dimensional ring of cells, updated synchronously, whose successive generations are successive rows of tissue. A cell holds the replicative ability r ∈ 0:R of the virion in it, the number of infective offspring it produces (0 for an empty cell). Cell i of the next generation receives the n = r₁ + r₂ offspring of cells i and i + 1:

  • with probability π^n every virion fails to infect it (host resistance), and it is empty;
  • otherwise one virion infects it, an offspring of the first parent with probability r₁ / n, and the offspring's ability is that of its parent minus one with probability p, plus one with probability q (only below R), and unchanged otherwise.

R is fixed when the model is built; π, p and q are parameters. With p = 0 and every cell at R this is directed bond percolation, and with only classes 0 and 1 the Domany–Kinzel automaton.

Fungus dispersal among permissive and resistant hosts

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_fungus_dispersal — Function
build_fungus_dispersal(; range=3.0, radius=59, seasonality=:none) -> CAModel

The fungus dispersal model of (Ferreira et al., 2013). Each cell is a host, permissive or resistant (the layer resistant), and healthy, latent or infectious (the layer host: 0, 1, 2), with clock the days since infection. In each daily step, synchronously:

  • a healthy host becomes latent with probability F (1 - ∏ᵢ (1 - f(dᵢ))^c), the product over every infectious host i at distance dᵢ, where f(d) = exp(-d / range) / (2π range²), F is F_permissive or F_resistant, and c is the daily propagule load of each infectious host;
  • a latent host becomes infectious latency days after it was infected;
  • an infectious host stays infectious.

range is the mean distance a propagule travels, in cells, and radius is the farthest distance the kernel reaches; the default reaches across the source's 60 × 60 lattice, so dispersal is global, as in the source. Both are fixed when the model is built. seasonality sets the daily load: :none, a constant load; :temperate, load_max * exp(-(day - peak)² / width²); :tropical, load_max * amplitude * (sin(2π day / period) + 1), where day = t + 1.

Citrus variegated chlorosis

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_cvc_citrus — Function
build_cvc_citrus(; kernel=cvc_flight_kernel(), method=:fft) -> CAModel

The citrus variegated chlorosis model of (Martins et al., 2000), in monthly steps. Each tree is healthy or diseased (diseased) and stressed or not (stressed; a diseased tree is stressed). With n_v(t) = n0 + trunc(n0 sin(2πt/T + φ)) flights from each diseased tree landing on offset o with probability kernel[o] (cvc_flight_kernel), a healthy tree is targeted with probability 1 - ∏ₓ (1 - K(y - x))^n_v(t) over the diseased trees x. A targeted tree is infected unless it is stressed, in which case it stays as it was; an untargeted healthy tree is stressed at the next step with probability 1 - p_nst(t), where p_nst(t) = p_min + (1 - 2p_min)(1 + sin(2πt/T + φ)) / 2. Diseased trees stay diseased.

The kernel is fixed when the model is built. method = :fft computes the targeting field by fast Fourier transform, which needs a periodic space: problem_cvc_citrus embeds the grove in a torus twice its size and masks the rest, so that nothing lies beyond the grove, as in the source.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_cvc_citrus — Function
problem_cvc_citrus(; grove=:SJ01, L=200, kernel=nothing, tspan=20, seed=0, pmap=(;),

The setup of (Martins et al., 2000) for one of its four groves (:SJ01, :SJ67, :SJ71, :SJ75, which set p_inf and p_min as in its Table 2): an L × L grove whose trees are diseased with probability p_inf (and then stressed) and otherwise stressed with probability 1 - p_nst(0), drawn from seed, for 20 months. The grove is the corner 1:L, 1:L of a 2L × 2L torus whose other cells are masked healthy trees, so flights leaving the grove are lost. kernel defaults to cvc_flight_kernel with radius L - 1.

Coffee leaf rust in fragmented landscapes

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_coffee_rust — Function
build_coffee_rust(; ca_rule=:archived) -> CAModel

The within-patch automaton of the coffee rust model of (Beasley et al., 2022). Each cell is matrix (0), with a canopy cover in the layer canopy, or a coffee farm, susceptible (1) or infected (2). A susceptible farm with N ≥ 1 infected Moore neighbours is infected in a step with probability scale (N + 1) / 10, the mean of the authors' code's scale × Beta(N + 1, 9 - N) draw (ca_rule = :archived), or N / 8, the mean of the paper's Beta(N, 8 - N) (ca_rule = :paper). Infected farms stay infected. Nothing lies beyond the edges.

Between-patch spread by spores is a step callback, coffee_rust_spore_callback, which problem_coffee_rust stores with the problem; it reads the parameters credit and spore_infection.

source

problem_coffee_rust(; n=120, buffer=10, clustering=0.2, deforestation=0.45, dispersion=3,

                    kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The simulated-landscape setup of (Beasley et al., 2022): an n × n landscape from coffee_rust_landscape, drawn from seed, with one coffee farm infected, for 1000 steps. spores is :intended (or true) for spores as the paper describes them (coffee_rust_spores!), :archived for spores as the authors' archived code moves them (coffee_rust_spores_archived!), or :none (or false). The first infected farm is chosen at random inside the buffer, or, with start = :anywhere (the default with archived spores, as in that code), anywhere in the landscape.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.coffee_rust_spores! — Function
coffee_rust_spores!(coffee, canopy, rng; credit=10.0, spore_infection=0.5) -> Int

One round of between-patch spread in the coffee rust model of (Beasley et al., 2022), in place: every infected farm with a matrix neighbour releases one spore into one of them, chosen at random; each spore in turn moves to a random admissible Moore neighbour (in range, matrix, canopy above zero), paying the canopy cover of each cell it enters, until its credit is spent or it cannot move; it then picks a random susceptible farm next to its end point, if any, and infects it with probability spore_infection. Returns the number of farms infected.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.coffee_rust_spores_archived! — Function
coffee_rust_spores_archived!(coffee, canopy, rng; credit=10.0, spore_infection=0.5,
                             stop=99, limit=50) -> Int

One round of between-patch spread as the authors' archived code (LandscapeSim.py) performs it, errors included, in place; written from a description of that code. With 0-based indices (r, c) on an n × n landscape:

  1. every infected farm, in row-major order, with a neighbour that is matrix or off the landscape picks one such neighbour at random, and keeps the spore there only if r < (n & c) < n (Python's a < b & c < d, with & the bitwise and), so spores off the top edge can be kept;
  2. each spore moves to random Moore neighbours that are matrix cells with canopy above zero, paying their canopy, while its credit is positive; it stops on entering a row or column above stop; a spore with no such neighbour moves by (1, 1) (Python 3.8's random.choices with zero weights), and entering coffee ends its walk;
  3. then each spore with a susceptible neighbour of its own end point picks one at random, but the target is that offset from the last spore's end point; if both target indices are below limit (negative ones wrap around, as in NumPy) the target is infected with probability spore_infection if it is susceptible.

Returns the number of farms infected.

Grapevine leafroll

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_grapevine_leafroll — Function
build_grapevine_leafroll(; undetectable=:duration) -> CAModel

The grapevine leafroll model of (Atallah et al., 2015), in monthly steps. Each cell is a vine with an infection state (state: 0 healthy, 1 exposed-undetectable, 2 exposed-detectable, 3 infective-moderate, 4 infective-high), an age in months (age), a clock (clock: the months left undetectable, or the months spent in an infective state), and flags set by the management callback (rogued, tested). Columns of the lattice are trellis rows. Each month, synchronously:

  • a healthy vine is infected with probability 1 - exp(-(nα α + nβ β)), where nα and nβ count the infective (3 or 4) neighbours along its trellis row and in the neighbouring rows;
  • with undetectable = :duration a new infection stays undetectable for a triangular number of months (eu_min, eu_mode, eu_max, rounded up); with :cdf, as the source writes it, an undetectable vine becomes detectable each month with probability F(x), the triangular distribution function at the months x it has been undetectable;
  • an exposed-detectable vine becomes infective-moderate with probability 1 - exp(-1/L), where L is L_young, L_mature or L_old by age (under 60 months, under 240, older);
  • an infective-moderate vine becomes infective-high with probability 1 - exp(-1/inf_period);
  • every vine ages by a month.

Management is grapevine_strategy_callback.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.grapevine_manage! — Function
grapevine_manage!(u, strategy; delay=0, maturity=36, protect_replants=true)
    -> (rogued, tested)

Apply a management strategy of (Atallah et al., 2015) to the vineyard state u in place, and flag the vines rogued and tested. Every vine meeting the trigger (:I, infective; :Im, infective-moderate; :Ih, infective-high; with an age class Y, M or O appended, only vines of that class) is rogued and replaced by a healthy vine of age 0. A spatial strategy (:ImNS, :ImNSEW, :ImNS2EW, :ImNS2EW2, and their age-class forms such as :ImM_NSEW) also tests the listed neighbours of each triggering vine that are not infective, once each, and rogues those found exposed-detectable. With protect_replants, vines younger than maturity months are never rogued. A vine triggers only once it has been infective for delay months, so that with delay = 1 it infects its neighbours for a month before it is found. Returns the numbers of vines rogued and tested.

Western X disease in a cherry orchard

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_western_x_orchard — Function
build_western_x_orchard() -> CAModel

The Western X disease model of (Ghorbani et al., 2026), in monthly steps. Each cell is a cherry tree with a disease state (0 an empty cell where a tree was cut, 1 unvisited, 2 visited by an infective leafhopper, 3 infected, 4 visible, 5 high), an age in months, a clock of months in its state, and flags set by the management callback (cut, replanted, tested). Each month, in every cell at once:

  • an unvisited tree is inoculated with probability alpha + beta k, where k counts the visible and high trees among its eight neighbours; with rates = :annual this is a yearly probability, converted to a monthly one;
  • a visited, infected or visible tree that has spent a whole number of years in its state moves to the next with probability progression;
  • every tree ages a month.

Management is western_x_manage!; the economics are western_x_economics.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_western_x_orchard — Function
problem_western_x_orchard(; strategy=:none, rows=20, cols=30, initial=(0.02, 0.08),
                          disease=true, years=25, seed=0, pmap=(;), kwargs...)
    -> CAProblem

The setup of (Ghorbani et al., 2026): a newly planted orchard of rows × cols trees (an acre), of which a share drawn uniformly from initial (a range, or a number), chosen at random from seed, start visited, for years years in monthly steps, under a management strategy (see western_x_manage!; :none for none). With disease = false, no tree is ever inoculated (the source's disease-free scenario). Nothing lies beyond the orchard. Management decides every other year, as in the source: from month 24 for threshold strategies and from month 48 for testing strategies, unless pmap says otherwise.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.western_x_manage! — Function
western_x_manage!(u, p, month, rng) -> u

The fixed management strategies of (Ghorbani et al., 2026), applied to the state u in place after the disease's step, in decision months (month at or after first_decision, every interval months). Visual scouting finds every visible and high tree.

  • :threshold_cut and :threshold_replant cut every symptomatic tree once at least threshold of the cells hold one (and replant it, as an unvisited tree of age 0).
  • :test_cut and :test_replant also test a share test_share of the trees within Moore radius radius of each symptomatic tree that are not symptomatic themselves; a test detects a visited or infected tree with probability sensitivity and never errs on an unvisited one. Symptomatic and detected trees are cut (and replanted).

The trees cut, replanted and tested are flagged for the month.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.western_x_economics — Function
western_x_economics(sol; price=2.35, discount=0.05, no_harvest=0.25, cut_cost=33,
                    plant_cost=14.3, test_cost=300, scale_costs=false) -> NamedTuple

The economics of a run saved every month, per orchard, as in (Ghorbani et al., 2026): each tree yields y(age) lb a month and costs c(age) dollars a month; the yield is multiplied by 1 - loss, with losses 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 in the visited, infected, visible and high states, and so is the cost with scale_costs = true, as the source's equation writes it; an empty cell yields and costs nothing. Each tree cut costs cut_cost, each replanted plant_cost more, each tested test_cost. Values are discounted at discount a year. Returns the yearly yield (lb) and net revenue (net, undiscounted), and the net present value npv.

Sudden oak death in California

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_sudden_oak_death — Function
build_sudden_oak_death(; short=sod_kernel(20.57; radius=4),
                       long=sod_kernel(9504; radius=99), method=:fft) -> CAModel

The sudden oak death model of (Meentemeyer et al., 2011), in weekly steps. Each cell holds S susceptible and I infected host units (at most nmax together), a forest type (forest: 0 none, 1 redwood-tanoak, 2 mixed evergreen) and whether it releases spores this week (sporulating). In week t, with weather index w_t (moisture times temperature suitability, from weather), every infected unit in a sporulating cell releases a Poisson number of spore units at rate beta w_t; each travels by the short-range kernel short with probability gamma and by the long-range kernel long otherwise (with the long-range share multiplied by long_factor from week management_week on); where it lands, in a cell whose forest is in season, it infects one of the S susceptible units with probability w_t S / nmax. New infections in a cell are therefore Poisson, capped at S. Redwood-tanoak forest takes part from January to September and mixed evergreen from February to September. method = :fft computes the long-range field by fast Fourier transform.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_sudden_oak_death — Function
problem_sudden_oak_death(; n=100, weather=:random, years=1990:2030, observed=1990:2008,
                         sites=3, wetness=0.5, management=nothing, seed=0, pmap=(;),
                         kwargs...)
    -> CAProblem

The setup of (Meentemeyer et al., 2011) on a synthetic landscape of n × n cells of 250 m: host units from a random cluster landscape (half the cells hold none), redwood-tanoak forest on about a third of the host cells and mixed evergreen on the rest, and sites host cells near the centre (within n / 8 cells of it, or the nearest host cells if there are too few there) each with one infected unit at the start of years. The weather of the observed years is drawn by sod_weather with wetness; later years repeat observed years at random (weather = :random), from the better half by weather index (:favourable) or from the worse half (:unfavourable), as in the source. management = (year, factor) multiplies the long-range share of dispersal by factor from that year on. All is drawn from seed. The landscape lies in the corner of a torus twice its size whose other cells are masked, so spores leaving it are lost; the state at time t is the end of week t.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.sod_kernel — Function
sod_kernel(scale; radius, cell=250, samples=16) -> Matrix{Float64}

The probability that a spore released at the centre of a cell lands in each cell within radius cells, for a Cauchy dispersal kernel of scale metres on cells of side cell metres: the spore travels in a uniform direction a distance from the half-Cauchy distribution 2 / (π scale (1 + (d/scale)²)), so that the kernel (1 + (d/scale)²)^-1 of (Meentemeyer et al., 2011) is the density per unit distance. The centre cell is integrated exactly; the others by the midpoint rule on samples × samples points. Spores landing beyond the radius are lost, so the probabilities sum to less than one.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.sod_temperature_index — Function
sod_temperature_index(x) -> Float64

The source's suitability of a week of mean temperature x °C for sporulation, fitted to Davidson et al. (2005): -0.066 + 0.056 x - 0.0036 (x - 15)² - 0.0003 (x - 15)³, at least 0.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.sod_weather — Function
sod_weather(rng; years=19, wetness=1, mean_temperature=13, temperature_range=4)
    -> Matrix{Float64}

Synthetic weekly weather for a coastal Californian forest, 52 × years: each week's index is its moisture (the share of its 7 days with rain) times its temperature suitability (sod_temperature_index). Temperatures follow a seasonal cycle about mean_temperature, warmest in August, with weekly noise; rain falls mostly from November to April, more in wet years (each year's wetness is wetness times a lognormal factor).
