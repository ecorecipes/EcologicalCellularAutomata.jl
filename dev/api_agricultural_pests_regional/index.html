iPyDisp dispersal of biological control agents

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_ipydisp_dispersal — Function
build_ipydisp_dispersal() -> CAModel

The dispersal automaton of iPyDisp (Agboka et al., 2024) on a graph of cells (a GraphSpace from ipydisp_neighbours). Each cell holds its status (0 unexposed, 1 exposed, 2 colonised) and whether it is suitable (it meets every constraint, see ipydisp_suitable). In each step, in every cell at once, a cell that is not colonised and has an exposed or colonised neighbour becomes colonised if it is suitable and exposed if not; other cells keep their status.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_ipydisp_dispersal — Function
problem_ipydisp_dispersal(; grid=nothing, neighbours=nothing, constraints=nothing,
                          start=nothing, steps=24, seed=0, kwargs...) -> CAProblem

A run of iPyDisp (Agboka et al., 2024) for steps time steps (months or years, as the user reads them): the cells in start (indices into the grid; iPyDisp takes the cells nearest the released sites) begin colonised. Without a grid, the setting is ipydisp_example drawn from seed; without start, the run starts from the cell nearest the grid's mean position. The model is deterministic.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.ipydisp_grid — Function
ipydisp_grid(; west, east, south, north, cellsize=1, inside=(lat, lon) -> true)
    -> (; lat, lon, row, col)

The cells of iPyDisp (Agboka et al., 2024): a grid over the bounding box with cells of cellsize × 0.00833 degrees (about cellsize km), numbered row by row from the north-west corner, cells at the east and south edges cut at the box. A cell is kept, at the centroid of its (cut) square, if inside holds there; iPyDisp keeps the cells meeting the region's polygon and places them at the centroid of their part inside it. Returns the kept cells' coordinates and their row and column in the grid.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.ipydisp_neighbours — Function
ipydisp_neighbours(lat, lon, travel) -> Vector{Vector{Int}}

The neighbourhood of iPyDisp (Agboka et al., 2024): for each cell, the other cells whose centroids lie less than travel km away (see ipydisp_distance), in increasing order. Pass it to GraphSpace.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.ipydisp_suitable — Function
ipydisp_suitable(constraints, n) -> Vector{Bool}

Whether each of n cells meets every constraint of iPyDisp (Agboka et al., 2024). constraints is a collection of (values, lower, upper), one per environmental layer, with a value per cell; a cell meets a constraint when lower < value <= upper. With no constraints every cell is suitable (the software's default bounds, -1 and 1, around a layer of zeros).

EcologicalCellularAutomata.ipydisp_example — Function
ipydisp_example(; cellsize=10, travel=15, seed=0) -> NamedTuple

A synthetic setting for iPyDisp: a box from 34° to 36° E and 1° S to 1° N (roughly central Kenya) cut to an ellipse, with one constraint, a smooth random layer in [0, 1] drawn from seed (a random cluster landscape, averaged over each cell's Moore neighbourhood), that cells must exceed 0.3 to be colonised; travel of 15 km joins each cell of about 9.3 km to its eight neighbours. Returns the grid, the constraints and the neighbours.

The tomato leafminer across Asia

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_tuta_absoluta_asia — Function
build_tuta_absoluta_asia(; radius=1) -> CAModel

The cellular automaton of (Guimapi et al., 2016) as (Guimapi et al., 2020) used it for Asia, on monthly steps: each land cell (land) is susceptible (0), exposed (1) or invaded (2) (state). In each month, in every cell at once,

  • a susceptible cell with an exposed or invaded cell among its Moore neighbours within radius cells becomes invaded if its mean temperature that month is at least temperature (°C) and its mean relative humidity at least humidity (%), and exposed otherwise;
  • an exposed cell becomes invaded in a month that meets both thresholds;
  • an invaded cell stays invaded.

The covariates temperature and humidity hold a slice per month. The layers reached and invaded record the month in which a cell was first reached and invaded (counting from 1; -1 if never). The vegetation index and tomato production of (Guimapi et al., 2016) are left out, as in (Guimapi et al., 2020).

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_tuta_absoluta_asia — Function
problem_tuta_absoluta_asia(; radius=1, origin=(38.4, 27.1), start_month=1, years=25,
                           seed=0, pmap=(;), kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The setup of (Guimapi et al., 2020): Asia on the grid of tuta_climate, susceptible everywhere but the cell at origin (degrees north and east; by default İzmir, near the first record in Turkey, in 2009), which is invaded, for years years of monthly steps from the month start_month. The covariates repeat the climatology every 12 steps. The model is deterministic.

EcologicalCellularAutomata.tuta_climate — Function
tuta_climate() -> NamedTuple

The monthly climate of Asia bundled with the model: CRU CL 2.0 mean temperature (°C) and relative humidity (%), 1961–1990 ((New et al., 2002)), averaged over cells of 1/3 degree on a grid of 267 rows from 78° N and 462 columns from 26° E. Returns temperature and humidity (267 × 462 × 12 arrays, January first, NaN off the land of the study area), land (a BitMatrix) and the latitudes and longitudes of the cell centres (lat, lon). The data are under the Open Database License (see models/tuta_absoluta_asia/data/NOTICE.md).

The western corn rootworm and crop rotation

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_rootworm_rotation — Function
build_rootworm_rotation() -> CAModel

The western corn rootworm model of (Szalai et al., 2014), in yearly steps on a torus of fields. Each cell holds its crop, the years it has been in maize (0 another crop, 1 first-year maize, 2 or more continuous maize), and the rootworm density pop (adults per plant). Each year, in every cell at once, continuous maize stays maize with probability pC (and its count rises), first-year maize with probability pF, and another crop turns to maize with probability pE (rootworm_probabilities); otherwise the field turns to another crop. With mandatory rotation (policy 1 to 3) a field that would be in maize for more than policy + 2 years turns to another crop. Growth and dispersal are rootworm_step!, a callback stored with the problem.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_rootworm_rotation — Function
problem_rootworm_rotation(; n=100, a=0.4, rotated=0.5, Q=1, policy=0, years=10, seed=0,
                          pmap=(;), kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The setup of (Szalai et al., 2014): a torus of n × n fields with a share a in maize, of which a share rotated is first-year (rotated) maize, crops drawn at random from their long-run shares, and initial rootworm densities in continuous maize drawn from a normal distribution of mean initial × threshold and standard deviation initial_sd times that (negative draws set to zero), all from seed. The state at time 0 is the first year after dispersal; the run lasts years years in all. The other parameters default to the source's most likely values (its Table 1). Throws an ArgumentError for a combination the crop dynamics cannot sustain.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.rootworm_probabilities — Function
rootworm_probabilities(a, b, Q, policy) -> NamedTuple

The yearly probabilities with which a field stays in or turns to maize in the model of (Szalai et al., 2014): pE that a field in another crop is sown with maize, pF that first-year maize stays maize, and pC that continuous maize stays maize, so that a share a of the fields is maize and a share b of the maize is continuous, with Q = pF / pC. With mandatory rotation after policy + 2 years of maize (policy 1, 2 or 3; 0 for none), pC solves the polynomial for the steady state, as in the source's script. Returns nothing if a probability falls outside [0, 1].

rootworm_rings(p) -> Int

EcologicalCellularAutomata.rootworm_step! — Function
rootworm_step!(u, p, rng; grow=true) -> u

The rootworm's year in the model of (Szalai et al., 2014), in place, after the crops have changed. If grow, populations outside continuous maize are lost, and those in continuous maize grow by a rate falling linearly from grA at grA_density to grB at grB_density, times the year's factor (uniform within year_effect of 1, the same for every field) and a normal factor of mean 1 and standard deviation growth_sd for each field. Then a share of the adults of each continuous maize field with maize within the range, rising linearly from emigration_min at emigration_min_density to emigration_max at emigration_max_density and times a normal factor of mean 1 and standard deviation emigration_sd (kept within the population), leaves, and lands in the maize fields within rootworm_rings fields (Chebyshev distance on the torus), in proportion to k - d + 1 at distance d. As in the source's script, densities below zero are set to zero.

Pesticide resistance across a region

EcologicalCellularAutomata.build_resistance_regional — Function
build_resistance_regional(; neighbourhood=:von_neumann) -> CAModel

The regional model of (Peck and Ellner, 1997), one generation a step. Each field holds N pests, a frequency q of the resistance allele, whether it is sprayed (a treated field rather than a refuge) or rotated, its carrying capacity K and its number of neighbours in the lattice. In every field at once:

  1. the population grows logistically, N + ⌊r (1 - N/K) N⌋ (at least 0);
  2. genotypes in Hardy-Weinberg proportions pay the cost of resistance (fitnesses 1, 1 - s h and 1 - s for susceptible homozygotes, heterozygotes and resistant homozygotes);
  3. a sprayed field whose population exceeds delta × Ks (Ks = k N0) is sprayed, killing a share rho of susceptible homozygotes and rho (1 - h) of heterozygotes;
  4. migrants leave and arrive at once: a share M of each field's population (rounded down), split equally among its neighbours in the lattice, carrying their field's allele frequency (migration = :total), or ⌊M N⌋ to each neighbour, as the source's equations read literally (migration = :per_neighbour);
  5. a rotated field is emptied every psi_period generations.

Neighbours are the von Neumann or Moore neighbours (neighbourhood); the lattice has hard edges.

source
EcologicalCellularAutomata.problem_resistance_regional — Function
problem_resistance_regional(; n=50, N0=1e7, k=2, kappa=0.5, q0=0.005, lambda=0.55, psi=0,
                            neighbourhood=:von_neumann, generations=250, seed=0, pmap=(;),
                            kwargs...) -> CAProblem

The setup of (Peck and Ellner, 1997): an n × n region of fields, a share lambda of them treated (drawn at random) and the rest refuges, and a share psi in rotation; carrying capacities Ks = k N0 in treated fields and kappa Ks in refuges; each field starting with ⌊N0 + σ⌋ pests, σ uniform on [-10⁶, 10⁶], and a frequency q0 of the resistance allele; all drawn from seed, for generations generations. The other parameters (r, delta, rho, s, h, M, migration, psi_period) default to the values of the source's figures and are set through pmap.
